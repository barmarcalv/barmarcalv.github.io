## The problem

Wikipedia's rules are mostly unwritten in the interface. A newcomer adds a paragraph in
good faith, does not cite a source, and the edit is undone within minutes — often by an
automated process, usually with a message they do not understand.

The rules were never hidden on purpose. They simply live in the community rather than in
the editing surface.

## Edit Check

Edit Check moves the rule to the moment it applies. When someone adds new content
without a source, the editor asks — while they are still writing, before they publish —
rather than letting the community explain afterwards with a revert.

The design question is not "how do we warn people". It is how to ask without making
someone feel caught, and how to stay out of the way of the thousands of experienced
editors who do not need the reminder.

## Suggestion Mode

Suggestion Mode brings a familiar idea from document editors into the VisualEditor:
propose a change rather than make it. It lowers the stakes of a first contribution, and
it gives experienced editors a way to work on articles where publishing directly would
be contentious.

## My part

Interaction design and interface work across both, inside a team that ships to a live
encyclopedia with an opinionated community attached to it. Every change is discussed in
public before it exists.
